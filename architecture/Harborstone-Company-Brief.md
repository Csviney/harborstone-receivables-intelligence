# Harborstone Response & Restoration

*Forward Deployed Engineering Case Study — Company Brief*

> **This is a fictional nationwide commercial restoration business. All company and stakeholder names are fictional.**

Harborstone began as a regional emergency-response contractor and has grown into a nationwide provider of commercial restoration, reconstruction, and roofing services. Its reputation was built on speed, reliability, and helping customers keep facilities operating after water, fire, storm, mold, or other property damage.

## The business

The company operates around the clock through a distributed network of regional branches. About 100 back-office employees support account development, estimating, project operations, coordination, accounts payable, accounts receivable, and finance. Field crews and subcontractors execute the work. Projects range from urgent mitigation jobs that begin before the full scope is known to long-running reconstruction projects with multiple bids, change orders, vendor invoices, and customer billing milestones.

## How work moves

A customer relationship or new loss begins in the CRM. Qualified opportunities move through estimating and proposal, then become active jobs in the project management system. Operations assigns ownership, manages budgets and documents, tracks progress, and approves project-related costs. The accounting system holds the financial books: vendor bills, customer invoices, payments, job costs, and period-close records. A project is not truly finished until operational completion, final costs, billing, collections, and closeout all agree.

## The current operating environment

The three systems were introduced at different times and were never designed as one workflow. Each uses its own identifiers, statuses, ownership fields, and definitions. CRM adoption varies by team; project data is richer but contains local conventions and incomplete handoffs; accounting is the controlled financial record but lacks much of the commercial and operational context. Email and spreadsheets fill the gaps. Leaders can get answers, but only after people export, match, explain, and reconcile data by hand.

## People in the room

- **Maya Chen, COO and executive sponsor:** Wants one trusted view of the business across regions and a clearer way to see where growth, delivery, or cash is getting stuck. She is interested in AI, but not in a demo that cannot survive contact with real operations.

- **Luis Ortega, VP of Operations:** Cares about urgent jobs starting quickly, ownership being clear, projects not stalling, and budget or margin issues surfacing early. Any solution must reduce administrative work for project teams.

- **Rachel Kim, VP of Finance:** Owns job profitability, AP/AR controls, working-capital visibility, and month-end confidence. She will challenge numbers that do not tie back to source records and expects assumptions and exceptions to be visible.

- **Devon Brooks, VP of Growth:** Wants to know which relationships are active, which opportunities are real, where conversion slows, and which customers deserve attention. He worries that tools fail when they ask salespeople to do extra data entry.

- **Tasha Green, Project Director:** Represents daily users. Her team works across branches, vendors, customer contacts, and finance. She will judge the result by whether it removes follow-ups and helps her act—not by how polished the technology looks.

## Why Ciridae is onsite

Harborstone employees have experimented with Claude and other AI tools and have seen that they can accelerate research, writing, and analysis. Leadership now wants AI embedded in the company's actual workflow, with the right business context and controls. After a successful sales process, Harborstone has engaged Ciridae to explore an end-to-end transformation. The team has already provided exports from its CRM, project management, and accounting systems. Today is the first onsite working session.

> **The immediate goal is not to replace every system or solve every problem. It is to show, by the end of the day, that Harborstone's disconnected data can support a credible and useful product experience—one that helps a real stakeholder or someone on their team understand the business, make a decision, complete important work, or take a meaningful next step, and creates confidence in the longer transformation.**
